# Proposed JovaBook tiers

Working proposal, 30 October 2026. This is a product decision document, not a purchasable offer or implemented entitlement system.

| Tier | Proposed price | Plain-language promise | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Essential | £20/month | Let customers book without the back-and-forth. | Core booking page, diary, clients, services and regular working hours. |
| Standard | £50/month | Keep your team's appointments together. | Essential plus separate staff sign-ins, permissions, team coordination and an SMS reminder option once implemented and tested. |
| Premium | Around £100/month | Your website and bookings, looked after together. | Standard plus a salon website connected to booking, with clearly agreed setup and maintenance scope. An optional AI booking assistant is a future Premium candidate. |

## Product rules

- Essential uses email reminders. SMS reminders belong to Standard and Premium, with an allowance and overage policy agreed before launch.
- Every tier should use the same simple booking journey. Add team controls when a business actually needs them.
- Do not sell upgrade value by making essential tasks harder or by adding menus to every account.
- Team records already exist, but separate staff access, permissions and tier limits are not enforced. Do not advertise them as delivered.
- Standard and Premium stay unavailable to subscribe until scope, costs and entitlements are approved.
- Existing Essential checkout remains a simulation and does not charge anyone.
- Do not include unlimited SMS, payment fees, custom development or unlimited AI in a plan promise before their costs and integrations are verified.
- Keep an AI assistant optional. A future assistant must use the real availability and booking rules, confirm before committing a booking, and provide an easy route to ordinary booking.
- Premium's website setup and ongoing work need a defined scope before confirming the price. Decide whether setup has a separate fee after estimating delivery time.

## Next decisions

Validate the team size and access needs with prospective businesses, cost the website setup and maintenance, then choose the smallest useful paid upgrade. Keep the current interface work separate from enabling billing or launching future tiers.
