# JovaMedia editorial brief

Write for a UK small business owner deciding how to improve a website or booking journey. Help the reader understand one specific problem, compare realistic choices and decide what to do next. Useful enquiries matter more than an arbitrary word count or a broad traffic target.

## Reusable writing prompt

Write an original JovaMedia article about the supplied topic and target search intent. First inspect the existing JovaMedia service, pricing and project information, then research current primary sources for any provider fees, product features, statistics or search guidance. Identify the customer's main question and what an ordinary search result fails to explain clearly.

Open with the question or a recognisable situation. Answer it directly in natural British English. Explain the choices through specific examples and connected paragraphs. Use a table when it makes genuine comparisons easier, and a list when the items are distinct checks. Choose a structure that suits this topic rather than repeating the last article's outline.

Include a worked example when it helps. Label assumed inputs, show the calculation and explain the conditions that affect the result. Separate acquisition commission, payment charges, software subscriptions and maintenance. Do not imply that a custom booking site replaces profitable marketplace discovery or includes every established platform feature.

Use the primary keyword naturally in the title, main heading or opening where appropriate. Write a useful description and descriptive internal links. Avoid repeated exact-match phrases, artificial borough lists and filler sections added to reach a length target. Link to the relevant JovaMedia service, pricing or actual project where it helps the reader assess the next step. End with an appropriate enquiry invitation, without hype or an invented discount.

Before publication, verify every factual number and scope claim. Add source links beside the information they support. Use the actual publication date, an accurate author byline and matching article schema. Keep important information readable as text rather than only inside an image.

## Voice and presentation

- Use clear, professional language. Be specific about what a website helps a customer do.
- Vary sentence length and paragraph structure naturally. Remove recurring slogans and repeated three-part rhythms.
- Do not use em dash characters, decorative eyebrows, slash-separated micro-labels, arrow flourishes or decorative counters such as `1/6`.
- Avoid forced introductions, generic conclusions, invented urgency and claims such as guaranteed rankings or guaranteed savings.
- Keep factual limitations beside the claim they qualify. Do not fill the article with administrative commentary about what it does not claim.
- Use real client evidence accurately. Distinguish a design concept, stock illustration and delivered project. Label a paraphrase as a paraphrase.
- Use visual material appropriate to the article. A treatment photograph, an actual website image and a cost comparison serve different purposes. Do not recolour the same poster six times.
- Keep one clear main heading, comfortable line lengths, accessible image descriptions and useful navigation.
